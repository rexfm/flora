Find

Look for soft, not split.

A ripe fig should feel heavy and yield gently at the neck. A little wrinkling is good; leaking or sour-smelling fruit has gone too far.

01

Toast + ricotta

Split figs over warm toast. Add ricotta, olive oil, salt and black pepper.

02

Salad + acid

Toss with bitter greens, walnuts and a sharp balsamic vinaigrette.

03

Warm + spoonable

Roast until slumped, then spoon over yogurt or vanilla ice cream.

The rule

Eat them quickly.

Figs do not improve after picking. Buy only what you can enjoy in a day or two.

03

Grow

A generous backyard tree.

Sun

Give it the warmest, sunniest place you have—ideally six or more hours.

Water

Water young trees deeply. Established figs tolerate some dryness, but steady moisture improves fruit.

Space

Plant where roots and branches have room. Containers can help control size.

Harvest

Pick only when fully colored, soft and drooping. Figs do not ripen well off the tree.
